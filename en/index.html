A student group with an open door

The BPSA brings together students and anyone interested, from many different disciplines, to think together about psychedelics, consciousness, science, philosophy and society.

We bring researchers working on altered states of consciousness to Berlin – from neuroscience to philosophy. Afterwards there’s time for questions and conversation.

More than talks: exchange, a film club and social events.

  • 3talks since September 2026, from neuroscience, psychology and philosophy
  • 65people in the lecture hall for Dr. Chris Timmermann’s talk

First time? Just drop by

  • Do I need to be a student?

    No. The talks are open to anyone interested in the topic.

  • Do I need prior knowledge?

    No. People from all kinds of fields come along, and questions are welcome.

  • Registration, cost?

    No registration, no fee. Just come a few minutes before the start.

  • Where exactly?

    Usually at HU Berlin, Campus Nord: Philippstraße 12, Haus 4, Hörsaal 4. The exact place is always listed with the talk.

  • Which language?

    German or English, depending on the speaker. It’s listed with the talk or announced with the topic in the WhatsApp group.

  • Can I join online?

    Some talks are streamed on Zoom; the link goes to the WhatsApp group. Recordings end up here under past talks.

08.09.Tue · 7 pm

BPSA Talk · in German

„Logos und Ekstase“

Miguel Estéfano Mora Vera · Philosophy, University of Freiburg

Logos and ecstasy: on the genealogy of an academic taboo

Recording, photos and what it was about

Miguel traced how the ecstatic became an academic taboo – from the Eleusinian Mysteries and Plato’s theia mania through the heresy trials to the habitus of today’s university.

Abstract and short bio

In today’s academic discourse, engaging with altered states of consciousness and psychedelic experiences still carries a whiff of the illegitimate. While the natural sciences laboriously rehabilitate the phenomenon through a purely biomedical, purpose-driven rationality, the habitus of the humanities largely avoids the ecstatic – for fear of breaking with the prevailing, sober and discursive logos.

This talk undertakes a search through the history of philosophy and shows that this taboo is not a timeless criterion of scientific rationality but the end product of two thousand years of repression:

Whereas in Greek antiquity philosophical insight and ecstatic dissolution of boundaries – from the Eleusinian Mysteries to Plato’s theia mania – stood in fruitful interplay, the institutionalisation of Christianity enforced the systematic criminalisation and exclusion of autonomous experiences of transcendence (from the condemnation of mysticism as heresy to the Malleus Maleficarum).

The modern university has inherited this apparatus of control and defence in largely secularised form. The talk argues for understanding the psychedelic once again as a genuine subject of epistemology and phenomenology, beyond trivialisation and biomedical reductionism – and outlines the need for an interdisciplinary framework that does justice to the human mind in its full noetic breadth.

About the speaker: Miguel Estéfano Mora Vera is a philosopher doing his PhD at the University of Freiburg, specialising in the existential legacy of Kierkegaard, Nietzsche and Heidegger. By bringing together Western and Eastern perspectives, he opens up new insights into the nature of reality and modern human existence. As an active musician and composer, he also regards the creative process as an essential extension of his philosophical work.

Partner

Psychedelic Student Network Germany

The BPSA is the Berlin group of the PSNG, Germany’s first nationwide student network for psychedelic science. It connects university groups across Germany and helps new ones get started.

  • Online lectures: every second Tuesday of the month at 7 pm on Zoom, free and open to everyone
  • Community calls and journal clubs with students from other cities
  • Most recently in Berlin: the PSNG × BPSA lecture with Prof. Dr. Timo Torsten Schmidt on 20 October
