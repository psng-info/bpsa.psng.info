Psychedelic research in Berlin — a map of the labs

Nine academic groups. Three institutions. Six of them within six hundred metres of each other.

This map shows every university research group in Berlin we could find that publishes peer-reviewed work on psychedelics or on altered states of consciousness — what each one actually studies, and how a student gets in. Last checked 19 October 2026.

Preliminary draft · corrections welcome: bpsa@psng.info

The groups

  1. 1

    Psychedelic Substances Research Group Charité – Universitätsmedizin Berlin · Department of Psychiatry and Neurosciences

    Who
    Led by Dr. med. Dimitris Repantis and Dr. med. Tomislav Majić, with around two dozen physicians, psychologists, philosophers and doctoral students.
    What they study
    Both ends of the subject at once. The therapeutic end: psilocybin, LSD, DMT and mescaline for depression, addiction, OCD and the distress of life-threatening illness; MDMA for PTSD; ketamine. And the end most clinics avoid — what happens when it goes wrong. Persisting perceptual changes (HPPD), depersonalisation, psychotic episodes, and harm reduction for people who use outside any clinic.
    Key work
    EPIsoDE, Germany's psilocybin trial for treatment-resistant depression, run with the Central Institute of Mental Health in Mannheim: 144 patients, three arms, and a negative primary endpoint — response was 17 % on 25 mg psilocybin against 10.6 % on active placebo (JAMA Psychiatry, 2026). Secondary measures did show a clinically meaningful drop in symptoms. Also the systematic review that defined the psychedelic "afterglow" window, and the cross-cutting PsychedELSI project on the ethical, legal and social side of the psychedelic renaissance (BMBF-funded, 2023–2026, coordinated here).
    Running now
    A trial of CYB003, a deuterated psilocin analogue, in major depression. PPaPS, on psychological symptoms that persist after psychedelic use. PsyTrans, comparing the ethics and culture of the new psychedelic therapies in Germany and Taiwan.
    How to get in
    If you have lasting effects after LSD, psilocybin, DMT, ayahuasca, mescaline or MDMA, PPaPS wants to hear from you — an anonymous online survey, about 20 minutes, open to anyone over 18. Clinically, Majić sees patients at the Charité psychiatric university clinic at St. Hedwig (the open ring on the map). No standing thesis advert; ask by email.

    Bonhoefferweg 3 (Charitéplatz 1), 10117 Berlin · Campus Charité Mitte

  2. 2

    Psychotropic Substances Research Group Charité – Universitätsmedizin Berlin · Department of Psychiatry and Neurosciences

    Who
    Headed by PD Dr. med. Stefan Gutwinski, Dr. med. Tomislav Majić and PD Dr. med. Inge Maria Mick. The sister group to number 1, and the wider one.
    What they study
    Psychoactive substances in general, with a stated emphasis on the illegalised ones: experimental behavioural and imaging studies of their effects on consciousness; who uses what and why, including new psychoactive substances; what drug policy, prevention and treatment actually achieve; dependence and comorbid psychosis; and the anthropological, historical and sociological side of the whole thing.
    Key work
    The scientific evaluation of Berlin's drug-checking programme — 1,120 samples from 530 people, of which only 44.8 % contained what the person thought they had bought, and 53.7 % held something unexpected. People who were told so were about five times more likely not to take it (final report October 2024, with the Frankfurt Institute for Addiction Research). It is the rare piece of psychedelic-adjacent research that changed a policy.
    How to get in
    The group currently lists nine doctoral candidates, three master's students and three research interns — so it does take people, and at every level. Write to the department and name the topic you want.

    Charitéplatz 1, 10117 Berlin · Campus Charité Mitte

  3. 3

    Research Group Recreational Drugs Charité – Universitätsmedizin Berlin · Department of Psychiatry and Neurosciences

    Who
    Headed by PD Dr. med. Felix Betzler.
    What they study
    The substances people take for pleasure rather than for a diagnosis — amphetamine, MDMA, cocaine, ketamine, cannabis, psilocybin, LSD. Prevalence, patterns and motives in the party scene, among students and in other subcultures; whether prevention campaigns work; and, separately, the therapeutic side.
    Key work
    Longitudinal survey work on Berlin students: personality change after a first psychedelic experience, following university students in Germany through it (npj Mental Health Research, 2026). Betzler also co-authored the EPIsoDE trial.
    How to get in
    Survey-based research, which means it genuinely needs participants and people to run them — the most approachable epidemiology on this map if you want a first project rather than a lab bench.

    Charitéplatz 1, 10117 Berlin · Campus Charité Mitte

  4. 4

    Larkum Lab Humboldt-Universität zu Berlin · Institut für Biologie, at Charité CrossOver

    Who
    Prof. Matthew Larkum, spokesperson of the collaborative research centre SFB 1315.
    What they study
    Not psychedelics — and that is exactly why it belongs here. The lab works on how a layer-5 pyramidal neuron combines the sensory input arriving at its body with the feedback arriving, far away, at the tip of its apical dendrite. Out of this came Dendritic Integration Theory (Aru, Suzuki & Larkum, Trends in Cognitive Sciences, 2020), which locates conscious processing in that coupling, and the finding that chemically unrelated general anaesthetics all converge on one mechanism: they decouple apical dendrites from the cell body (Cell, 2020).
    Why it is on this map
    Because the current cellular accounts of psychedelics are built on top of it. 5-HT2A receptors sit densely on precisely those apical dendrites, and the 2026 proposals — "apical amplification", "apical hypercontextualisation" — are attempts to explain a psychedelic as an apical-drive problem. The lab itself has published nothing on psilocybin or LSD. It supplies the machinery the theory borrows.
    Key work
    Active cortical dendrites modulate perception (Science, 2016). Currently funded by an ERC Advanced Grant, Dendro-somatic coupling and global neuronal signalling — €2.5 million over five years, to work out the basis of anaesthesia in mammals.
    How to get in
    No standing advert; this is an in-vivo electrophysiology lab, so expect to need a reason. matthew.larkum@hu-berlin.de

    Charité CrossOver, Virchowweg 6 (Charitéplatz 1), 10117 Berlin

    larkumlab.com SFB 1315

  5. 5

    Schmitz Lab, Neuroscience Research Center Charité – Universitätsmedizin Berlin · with NeuroCure, DZNE Berlin and the Bernstein Center — same building as 4

    Who
    Prof. Dr. Dietmar Schmitz, director of the Neuroscience Research Center (NWFZ) since 2005, spokesperson of the NeuroCure Cluster of Excellence since 2015, of DZNE Berlin since 2011 and of the Einstein Center for Neurosciences since 2016 — and Berlin's first Einstein Professor, in 2011. The psychedelic work in the lab is carried by Dr. Roberto De Filippo.
    What they study
    Synapses and circuits of the hippocampus and the entorhinal cortex: how a memory is encoded, consolidated and lost. Memory, not psychedelics, is the lab's day job. Its psychedelic line asks the mechanistic question sitting underneath almost everything else on this map — what psilocybin actually does to a cortical circuit, cell by cell.
    Why it is on this map
    Half for the science, half for the plumbing. The research centre, the cluster of excellence and the graduate school that most of the Mitte cluster sits inside are ones he speaks for, and he put his own name on a psychedelics paper in 2024 — which in German neuroscience counted as early.
    Key work
    De Filippo & Schmitz, Synthetic surprise as the foundation of the psychedelic experience (Neuroscience & Biobehavioral Reviews, 2024): a psychedelic works by manufacturing a prediction error through 5-HT2A receptors — a surprise with nothing behind it. Since July 2024 that idea has been under test in mice in an Allen Institute OpenScope project run with Torben Ott (number 6) and De Filippo, which is where the two 2026 psilocybin preprints on number 6's card come from.
    How to get in
    No adverts; the lab keeps a standing lab rotation page instead, and the doctoral route into basic neuroscience in Mitte is the Einstein Center for Neurosciences programme. inbox-schmitzlab@charite.de

    Charité CrossOver, Virchowweg 6, Level 03 (Charitéplatz 1), 10117 Berlin · same building as 4

    schmitz.neurocure.de Lab rotations Synthetic surprise

  6. 6

    Decision Circuits Lab Humboldt-Universität zu Berlin · Bernstein Center for Computational Neuroscience & Institut für Biologie

    Who
    Dr. Torben Ott, who started the group at the Bernstein Center with DFG Emmy Noether funding after a postdoc with Adam Kepecs.
    What they study
    Decisions written out as explicit algorithms, in rats, mice and humans at the same time — and how dopamine and serotonin retune the cortical circuits that run them. The psychedelic thread is stated outright among the lab's four research lines: how drugs alter these computations, and how they produce subjective states such as hallucinations.
    Key work
    With Katharina Schmack and Adam Kepecs, a way to measure hallucination-like perception in mice and in people with the same task, showing a causal role for striatal dopamine (Science, 2021), plus the companion argument for doing computational psychiatry across species (JAMA Psychiatry, 2021). In 2026, two preprints with the Allen Institute: psilocybin collapsing visual change detection and pushing cortex toward a state of surprise, and a brain-wide reconfiguration of burst firing under psilocybin that depends on the 5-HT2A receptor.
    How to get in
    The clearest answer on this whole map. The lab accepts master's thesis projects — experimental, computational or both — and one of the four advertised topics is how drugs produce subjective states such as hallucinations. Email Ott with a short motivation and CV.

    Philippstraße 13, Haus 18, 10115 Berlin · +49 30 2093 98520

  7. 7

    Lueken Lab — the psychedelic strand Humboldt-Universität zu Berlin · Institut für Psychologie, Campus Adlershof

    Who
    Prof. Dr. Ulrike Lueken and Dr. Ricarda Evens, who also sits in the Charité group above. Part of the German Centre for Mental Health (DZPG), Berlin-Potsdam site, and one floor away from number 8, with whom they publish.
    What they study
    An anxiety- and trauma-first clinical psychology department with a serious psychedelic strand running through it: what happens in the days and weeks after a psychedelic, what to do when that window goes badly, and whether non-drug routes arrive at the same place.
    Key work
    The systematic review that put a name and a shape on the "afterglow" — the subacute effects of classic serotonergic psychedelics (Therapeutic Advances in Psychopharmacology, 2023). A naturalistic survey of PTSD symptoms following psychedelic use (Psychological Medicine, 2026). A landscape analysis of every pre-registered classical-psychedelic trial (Journal of Psychopharmacology, 2025). And an expert evaluation of high-ventilation breathwork as a non-pharmacological model for psychedelic-like states (npj Mental Health Research, 2026).
    How to get in
    A teaching department, so Bachelor's and Master's theses run through it in the normal way — and the surveys above recruit online, which is the lowest-friction way to contribute to psychedelic research in this city.

    Rudower Chaussee 18, 12489 Berlin (visiting) · postal: Unter den Linden 6, 10099 Berlin

    Institut für Psychologie DZPG Berlin-Potsdam Directions

  8. 8

    Clinical Psychology of Social Interaction — the breathwork line Humboldt-Universität zu Berlin · Institut für Psychologie, Campus Adlershof

    Who
    Prof. Dr. Isabel Dziobek, with the breathwork work carried by Nina Hartter, Maria Badanova, Barbara Breitenstein and colleagues. Also at the Berlin School of Mind and Brain and the DZPG Berlin-Potsdam site — and in the same building as number 7.
    What they study
    The lab's day job is social interaction: autism, social anxiety, personality disorders. Its altered-states line is high-ventilation breathwork — breathing deliberately faster and deeper than usual until the state changes. No substance, no licence, no narcotics law. And a dose you can put down mid-session, simply by breathing normally again, which is something no psychedelic offers.
    Key work
    Forty-eight mental health professionals went through a breathwork workshop and rated the states they reached as comparable in intensity to moderate doses of serotonergic psychedelics. They proposed internalising disorders as indications, and named psychotic, dissociative and bipolar disorders as contraindications (Lueken, Dziobek et al., npj Mental Health Research, 2026 — a paper shared with number 7). A global survey of practitioners then found that the less people avoided what came up during a session, the better they felt in the days afterwards (Erdmann et al., Frontiers in Psychology, 2026).
    Running now
    Exhaling the past, a neuropsychological test of breathwork as a tool for processing trauma, measured against progressive muscle relaxation (Tiny Blue Dot Foundation, since 2024). And BreathDep, newly funded in the DZPG VISIONS26 line in February 2026.
    How to get in
    Two open doors, and the widest on this map. The chair invites Master's theses outright — current topics are posted in the KliPP Moodle. And "Exhaling the past" is recruiting people who have never done breathwork or progressive muscle relaxation before, so inexperience is the qualification.

    Rudower Chaussee 18, 12489 Berlin · Campus Adlershof, same building as 7

    dziobek-lab.org Projects Directions

  9. 9

    Professorship of Cognitive Neuroscience MSB Medical School Berlin · Faculty of Natural Sciences, Wilmersdorf

    Who
    Prof. Dr. Timo Torsten Schmidt — who also sits in the Charité group (1), teaches at the Freie Universität, and was our speaker on 20 October 2026.
    What they study
    The measurement problem underneath all of this: how do you compare one altered state to another at all? Schmidt built the Altered States Database, which pulled the psychometric questionnaire data out of 165 published studies of pharmacological and non-pharmacological altered states, so that an LSD dose, a meditation retreat and a flicker lamp can finally be put on the same axes (Scientific Data, 2022; open data on OSF).
    Key work
    Dose–response relationships for psilocybin's subjective effects, built from that database. Classification schemes of altered states (2025). And the drug-free arm of the same question, run with Till Nierhaus and Ioanna Amaya at the Freie Universität: stroboscopic light as a route into visual hallucinations, where thalamocortical coupling scales with how intense the phenomena get (Imaging Neuroscience, 2023; Network Neuroscience, 2025). The 2015 paper with Majić and Gallinat on peak experiences and the afterglow, which asked early whether the therapeutic effect depends on the experience — still one of the field's open questions. Plus the Altered Xperience citizen-science project.
    How to get in
    The database is open. If you want to do psychedelic research in Berlin this week rather than next year, the honest answer is to download the ASDB and ask it something nobody has asked it yet. For the longer route, Schmidt coordinates the FU's M.Sc. Cognitive Neuroscience — the most direct degree into altered-states research in this city.

    Rüdesheimer Straße 50, 14197 Berlin

How this map was made, and what is missing from it

The rule we used: a university research group in Berlin that publishes peer-reviewed work on psychedelics or on altered states of consciousness. Everything here was checked against the group's own pages and its publications, not against press coverage. Where a group's psychedelic connection is indirect, we say so on its card rather than quietly inflating it.

  • Deliberately not here: the MIND Foundation, OVID Clinic Berlin, atai Life Sciences and the other Berlin companies, therapy-training providers, and drug-checking as a service rather than as research. All of them matter to psychedelics in Berlin. None of them is an academic research group, which is what this map is about.
  • Not in Berlin: the Central Institute of Mental Health in Mannheim, which is the other half of EPIsoDE and arguably the centre of gravity of German psychedelic medicine.
  • People who left: Katharina Schmack, whose hallucinating-mice work was done with Berlin co-authors, now runs her own lab at the Francis Crick Institute in London. The Berlin scene is small, and some of its best-known names are in it only by alumni ties.
  • One address, several groups: numbers 1, 2 and 3 are three separate research groups inside one Charité department at one address, and 7 and 8 are two chairs in one building in Adlershof — which is why those pins read 1–3 and 7–8 rather than carrying a single number. Numbers 4 and 5 share the Charité CrossOver building too, but belong to different universities, so they keep their own colours and sit shoulder to shoulder instead.
